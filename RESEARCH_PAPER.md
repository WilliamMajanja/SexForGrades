# Research Paper: Patronage, Extortion and the Corruption of Computer Science at Kenya Methodist University (KeMU)

**Author:** William Majanja (CIS-1-4767-3/2013)  
**Institutional Focus:** Kenya Methodist University, Department of Computer Science  
**Date:** November 2026  

## Abstract

This paper documents firsthand evidence of patronage, extortion, and academic corruption within Kenya Methodist University's Department of Computer Science. Drawing from personal experience as a student (Registration No. CIS-1-4767-3/2013), it exposes how a bribe of 500,000 Kenyan Shillings was solicited in exchange for a PhD, alongside the systematic weaponisation of missing marks to suppress students in the pure sciences. Operating under GEMA patronage whilst marketing itself as a "Christian university", KeMU's Computer Science Department has been captured by a patronage network that prioritises connections over merit, victimises those who question, and actively suppresses the sciences.

## 1. Introduction

Kenya Methodist University presents itself as a faith-based institution committed to Christian values and academic excellence. However, material evidence and firsthand experience reveal a stark contradiction between its public image and internal practice. For students pursuing Computer Science and other pure sciences, the department has become a site of obstruction, intimidation, and extortion rather than learning and merit-based advancement.

## 2. Methodology

This research relies primarily on firsthand testimony from the author (William Majanja, CIS-1-4767-3/2013), supplemented by observed patterns affecting fellow students in the Computer Science Department. It documents concrete incidents, including the solicitation of a 500,000 KES bribe in exchange for a PhD, and the systematic weaponisation of missing marks.

## 3. Findings

### 3.1 Solicitation of a 500,000 KES Bribe for a PhD

In the course of academic interactions within Kenya Methodist University's Computer Science Department, the author was made aware of and directly encountered an attempt to solicit **a bribe of Five Hundred Thousand Kenya Shillings (KES 500,000)** in exchange for consideration towards a PhD. This solicitation represents a gross violation of academic integrity, the Universities Act, CUE regulations, and basic principles of meritocracy. It demonstrates that advancement to doctoral level has been commodified, with access contingent on financial bribes rather than academic competence, research capability, or peer review.

### 3.2 Weaponisation of Missing Marks

A consistent pattern observed was the deliberate withholding of marks for Computer Science and other science units. Students pursuing the pure sciences were disproportionately affected by:
- **Missing results**: Units marked as "missing", "not entered", or perpetually "under review" for extended periods
- **Delayed processing**: Marks withheld until students engaged in repeated office visits
- **Bureaucratic runarounds**: Bounced between lecturers, exam officers, and faculty with contradictory excuses
- **Obstruction of graduation**: Missing marks used to delay clearance and graduation despite completed coursework

### 3.3 Patronage, GEMA Networks and Science Suppression

KeMU's capture by GEMA patronage networks has created a two-tier system:
- **Connected students**: Protected, fast-tracked, and shielded from consequences
- **Unconnected science students**: Subjected to obstruction, gaslighting, and extortion
- **Theology over science**: Resources and protection flow disproportionately to "safe" courses whilst the Computer Science Department is starved of accountability

### 3.4 Institutional Hypocrisy

KeMU markets itself as a Christian university preaching morality whilst presiding over a system of bribery and extortion. The contradiction between stated values and material practice is stark. Those who preach Christian ethics are complicit in commodifying education and preying on students.

## 4. Analysis

The Computer Science Department at KeMU has been captured by a patronage clique. Merit has been subordinated to connections. Education has been commodified - students are expected to pay not just fees, but bribes for marks and advancement. The solicitation of 500,000 KES for a PhD is not an isolated aberration, but symptomatic of a systemic culture of corruption enabled by impunity and lack of accountability.

Science students are particularly targeted because a technically competent graduate body threatens the patronage system. An independent, critical mass of computer scientists cannot be easily controlled or extorted.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Independent audit**: CUE, KNQA and relevant authorities must conduct an independent forensic audit of the Computer Science Department's results, admissions, and postgraduate processes.
2. **Investigation of bribery**: The solicitation of 500,000 KES for a PhD must be formally investigated with protection for witnesses.
3. **Missing marks amnesty**: All students affected by missing marks must have their results audited and cleared without further extortion.
4. **Whistleblower protection**: Strong protections for students exposing corruption.
5. **Accountability**: Those found culpable must face sanctions including dismissal and prosecution.
6. **Student representation**: Genuine, independent student representation with real power to oversight.

## 6. Conclusion

The Kenya Methodist University Computer Science Department, as experienced by William Majanja (CIS-1-4767-3/2013), represents the commodification of education and the capture of institutions by patronage and corruption. The demand for a 500,000 KES bribe for a PhD is a smoking gun exposing the rot. This cannot be addressed through internal complaints channels controlled by those implicated. It requires external, independent intervention and mass student action.
