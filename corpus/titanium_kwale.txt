Titanium Mining and Resettlement in Kwale

The Kwale mineral sands project became one of the largest mining investments in
Kenya. Its development required the acquisition of land and the relocation of
communities from the Maumba and Nguluku areas to make way for the mine.

Residents and civil society organisations have raised persistent concerns about
compensation, the adequacy of resettlement land, and the transparency of the
agreements that governed the relocation. Reporting has documented disputes over
household counts, payment schedules and access to replacement farmland.

As the mine approaches closure, attention has turned to the restoration of
mined-out sites and to the long-term land use of the surrounding area. Independent
monitoring, transparent benefit-sharing and enforceable environmental obligations
are all essential if the legacy of extraction is not to be borne mainly by the
communities who were displaced.
