Elephant Conservation in Coastal Kenya

The Arabuko-Sokoke Forest supports one of the most important remaining populations
of the endangered African forest elephant in East Africa. A 2017 dung-count survey
estimated approximately 215 elephants within the forest, with a wide confidence
interval that reflects the difficulty of counting animals in dense vegetation.

Human-elephant conflict has increased as farmland expands into traditional movement
corridors. Community members report that elephants raid maize, mango and banana
farms, particularly during the dry season when water and forage are scarce. Where
corridors are blocked, elephants are forced to cross cultivated land, which raises
the risk of crop damage and retaliatory killing.

Effective conservation therefore depends on protecting movement corridors, improving
community benefit-sharing, and monitoring populations with methods that acknowledge
their uncertainty rather than presenting a single number as exact.
